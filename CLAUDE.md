# Bleeders

The game: a third-person narrative RPG/adventure with an orthographic 2.5D look (3D scenes with hand-drawn textures and a 2D feel), point and click, player-paced (no decay timers or forced timings). Lighting is the most important part of the look.

It is built on the FlickerVoid engine (FlickerVoidNGN), one clone of which every project shares, named by the `FLICKERVOID_ENGINE` environment variable. The engine's `CLAUDE.md` describes the stack, the engine modules, the editor and the conventions; read it before working here, as its rules (Arch-first entities, data-driven content, the ontology tree and the rule of three, porting from Unreal, code style, TDD, test builders) apply to this repository too. The plan and roadmap are the engine's `Docs/Plan.md` and `Docs/Port.md`.

## Engine

- `Directory.Build.props` sets `$(FlickerVoidEngine)` to the `FLICKERVOID_ENGINE` environment variable (the build stops when it names no engine) and imports the engine's `Directory.Build.props` and `Directory.Packages.props`. There is never a copy of the engine per project. Projects reference the engine's projects (`$(FlickerVoidEngine)Source/Engine/<Module>/...`), not its packages, so every build compiles the engine. `Bleeders.slnx` lists only the game's projects, as a solution can't read the variable; engine code is edited in the engine's own solution.
- The game builds against whatever the engine clone holds; nothing pins a commit. Engine changes are made and merged in FlickerVoidNGN; a change that spans both lands in the engine first. CI checks the game and the engine out side by side, the engine at `ENGINE_REF` (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`): main, or an engine pull request's branch while a game change waits on it.
- Projects can sit anywhere: the editor's project picker remembers every project it opened (Browse to `Bleeders.fvproject` once).

## Layout

- `Bleeders.fvproject` opens the game in the editor (`game`: `Source/Launcher/Bleeders.Launcher.csproj`; `startupScene`: `Scenes/Main.fvscene`). `Source/Launcher` runs it on `Content/Scenes/Main.fvscene`.
- `Source/<Module>/Bleeders.<Module>.csproj` (`Gameplay`, `Interface`, `Launcher`), tests in `Tests/<Module>/Bleeders.<Module>.Tests.csproj`, folders following the ontology tree (`Gameplay/Protagonist/Identity/Social`), namespaces following folders. Game-specific rules (social, quests), content and their tests live here, never in the engine; generic systems go to the engine.
- `Source/Gameplay/GameplayModule.cs` is the game's `IGameModule`, registering its components, systems and hooks; `Serialization/BleedersJsonContext.cs` is its source-generated JSON context. The launcher adds the module to `SceneHost.Game` and saves under `FlickerVoid/Bleeders` in the user's local application data.
- Inside the `Bleeders` namespaces, engine types are named in full (`FlickerVoid.Engine.Runtime.Components.Transform`), never as `Engine.…`.

## Game

- Walking to what was clicked is the game's controller's job, over `Characters`: `ClickControlSystem` (`Gameplay/Protagonist/Control`, over the `Player` entity class: protagonist, interactor, mover, transform) turns a press of `Input.Use` into a ray from the camera, walks the `protagonist` there, or focuses the interactable it hit, walks within reach and presses Use (and ignores clicks while the protagonist is in a conversation), its `WalkControlSystem` turns the `Input.Move` action (WASD) into a `SteerCommand` on the ground relative to the camera, held still during a conversation, its `InteractableHighlightSystem` outlines the interactables the protagonist's interactor could use within its `interactableHighlight` range (`InteractableHighlight`: range, color) and takes the outline off the rest, and its `DoorSwingSystem` (`Gameplay/World/Objects/Doors`, over the `Door` entity class) swings a `doorSwing` door about its hinge as its toggle changes and rattles it when locked (a `NoiseEvent` from whoever tried it); its `KeySystem` (`Gameplay/Protagonist/Resources/Possessions`) hears a toggle blocked by a lock first and, when whoever was blocked carries an item with the lock's `Key` tag, unlocks it (taking the key when the lock consumes it) and lets the toggle go ahead. The main scene has a cellar key pickup the fox takes before the door opens. Residents (`Gameplay/World/Society/Residents`, in place of the Unreal StateTree AI): a `resident` (wander radius, rest) is run by `ResidentSystem` (over the `Dweller` entity class: resident, state, transform), which faces whom it talks to, stops to watch whom it sees until it loses sight of them, turns toward noises it hears, and otherwise walks to random points within its wander radius of home, resting between walks; the innkeeper watches the fox and turns at the rattling door.
- The street (`Main.fvscene`, the `Street` folder, greyboxes for now; owner 2026-11-23): the inn (the room as its ground floor, a closed upper storey and a roof), a two-storey house (a door, windows, stairs up through the ceiling) and a shack with a broken roof over a wine cellar (stairs down through its floor, a lantern, the outside muted). Each building is an empty entity with its storeys nested as indoor volumes (dark inside but for windows, holes and lamps) and their pieces nested under them, so the engine's `CutawaySystem` opens the storey the fox stands in. The inn's room model has no walls to walk into, so unseen static bodies (`Inn Wall ...`) close it but for its front entrance. Yellow `doorway` outlines (the `Doorways` folder) mark every entrance and stair head, and the cellar door is `uncut`, so the cutaway never hides it. The `Street` shot tracks the fox along the street, and the `Inn Inside` and `Inn Outside` cues switch to and from the room shot. `PointerPicker` passes clicks through what the cutaway hides (`CutawaySystem.IsUnseen`). `StreetTests` (`Tests/Gameplay/World/Buildings`) walk the fox out, upstairs and into the cellar, keep the inn open once it stops and keep it inside the inn's walls when steered.
- The HUD minimap draws the minimap view's markers (the tracked one as a diamond); `Maps/Inn.fvdata` covers the inn, the innkeeper is a `Person` marker and the cellar door an `Objective` shown while the cellar quest is active.
- The launcher quick saves to the `Quick` slot with `Input.QuickSave` (F5) and loads it with `Input.QuickLoad` (F9) (`QuickSaves`). The stage 7 gate is the project's `StoryLoopTests` (`Tests/Gameplay/Story`), which load the source `Main.fvscene` headless and play the cellar key quest through clicks and choices, with a save and load in the middle.
- The story: the protagonist is the `Player` of `QuestSystem` and `KnowledgeSystem` while it is active (`GameplayModule`), the innkeeper in `Main.fvscene` has a Talk offer (`Interactables/Talk.fvdata`, `startConversation`) and the dialogue `Dialogues/Innkeeper.fvflow`, which gives the cellar key quest (`Quests/Cellar.fvdata`) and a topic (`Knowledge/Cellar.fvdata`). The launcher's debug interface (`--debug` or F1) also has a Story panel (`StoryWindow`: the line, the choices to pick, quests and knowledge).
- The rules: the fox has skills (Perception, Stealth, Charisma) and physical attributes (Health, Intoxication) in `Main.fvscene` and the `Abilities/Protagonist.fvdata` set with `Ability.Lockpick` (needs an `Item.Lockpick`). Its `LockpickSystem` (`Gameplay/World/Objects/Doors`, beside the door) hears a toggle blocked by a pickable lock that no key opened (after `KeySystem`), activates the ability on the door and rolls `Checks/Lockpick.fvdata` (Perception, 2d6, +1 unless `Status.Drunk`) against 4 plus a tenth of the lock's difficulty; success opens it like a key (`DoorLocks.Unlock`), failure cancels the ability, and `LockpickedEvent` carries the result. A mug of ale on a crate (`Items/Ale.fvdata`, drunk where it stands) adds Intoxication and the `Drunk` modifier. The launcher's debug interface has a Rules panel (`RulesWindow`: attributes with their bases, modifiers, abilities and the last check).
- The scanner (`Gameplay/Protagonist/Resources/Knowledge/Scanning`, ported from the Unreal scanner for point and click): a `scanner` (range, eye height, line of sight, update interval) on the protagonist and `scannable`s (a `ScanDefinition` data asset, type `scan`: display, category, scan duration, `ScannableWhen`, up to three shown `ScanEntry`s each with `VisibleWhen`, `OnScanned` applied on the first completed scan; and the height the scanner looks at). `ScannerSystem` (a fixed system and service, added where there is a window, as it focuses through `PointerPicker`) turns scan mode on and off with `Input.ScanMode` (F), reveals the scannables within range whose conditions pass, focuses the revealed one under the pointer when the scanner sees it, and scans it while `Input.Scan` (the right button) is held (`ScanModeChangedEvent`, `ScanRevealChangedEvent`, `ScanFocusChangedEvent`, `ScannedEvent`). Scan mode does not slow time (the game is player-paced) and revealed scannables are not yet highlighted in the world. The HUD's scan card (`ScanCardViewModel`) takes the prompt's place in scan mode: the focus's title and progress, then its visible entries. The door, the innkeeper and the ale have scans (`Scans/`).
- The camera direction (`Main.fvscene`, its shots and cue areas in the `Cameras` folder): the `Camera` entity is the starting room shot with the `Cameras/Room.fvdata` rig (a small follow and look, shake, zoom punch); the `Window Spot` area blends to the `Window` shot and plays it (turning about the room and zooming out) and leaving it blends back; with the key found (`Found.CellarKey`, set by the key's own `Interactables/CellarKey.fvdata` pickup) the `Cellar Stairs Enter` area fades and pushes the low `Cellar` shot at the door, and leaving `Cellar Stairs Exit` returns with a fade (both nested under the empty `Cellar Stairs Trigger`, so they move together); and the cellar quest's flow (`Quests/Cellar.fvflow`) plays the skippable `Key Reveal` cutscene once the key is found (pushed with a cross-fade, played without layers, returned with a blend; `Cutscene.KeyFound` keeps it from playing twice). The `Camera` entity is also the `mainCamera`, whose depth fade (nearer than the fox darker, farther melting into a warm haze) every shot draws with; the `Cellar` shot overrides its near side to darken the low foreground more. The fox is the `cameraTarget` and `triggerActivator`. `CameraControlSystem` (`Gameplay/Protagonist/Control`, where there is a window) gives the director the pointer and skips with `Input.Skip` (Space). `CameraDirectionTests` play these on the source scene; the story loop's clicks aim the live shot's drawn view.
- The interface (`Source/Interface`, `Bleeders.Interface`: `InterfaceApplication` with Fluent and `Themes/GameTheme.axaml`, AXAML views over CommunityToolkit.Mvvm view models of the game's services) is drawn by the launcher's `GameUi` under the debug interface (`FrameOverlays`). `GameInterface` (a frame system) owns the HUD (`HudViewModel`, `HudView`): the quest tracker at the top left (`QuestTrackerViewModel`: up to three active quests, the latest changed first, each with its first visible objective not done), the dialogue box at the bottom centre while talking (`DialogueBoxViewModel`: speaker, line, Next, the numbered choices with locked reasons, Leave), and otherwise the prompt for the interactable under the pointer (`InteractionPromptViewModel` over `InteractionSystem.GetOffers(interactor, target)`; the pointer is found by `PointerPicker`, the game service `ClickControlSystem` picks with). Only the dialogue box takes clicks. The look is white and tintable (owner, 2026-11-21: serious, white, tint set in the engine): `HudTint` and the brushes in `GameTheme.axaml`, `HudFrame` panels (`Controls/HudFrame.cs`: a chamfered outline with corner ticks), headings in Glitch Goblin and text in Rajdhani (SIL OFL only; licences beside the fonts in `Assets/Fonts`), and the minimap at the bottom right (`MinimapViewModel`: the compass ring turned against the camera's heading, a tiled map grid moving under the player, the view cone turned by the player's facing, and the markers of `MapSystem.BuildMinimapView`; images in `Assets/Images`). The hub (`HubViewModel`, `HubView`, over the HUD, which hides while it is open) replicates the Unreal hub: a yellow bar with the skewed `FadingStrips` (an opacity mask), the page tabs and Close, and the selected page; `Input.Hub` (Tab) and Close toggle it (Escape still closes the game's window, in `SceneRenderSystem`), `Input.NextPage` (E) and `Input.PreviousPage` (Q) switch pages in a ring, and it reopens on the page last shown. Its dark background takes every click. Its three pages (`HubPageViewModel`: filled from the protagonist when shown, refreshed by their events while shown) are Inventory (`InventoryPageViewModel`: weight and money, a four-column grid of the carried stacks filled up to the inventory's cells, the selected stack with Use and Wear or Take off, and the outfit slots in two columns, `OutfitSlots`; clicking a slot wears the selected stack there when it fits, else selects what is worn there; the protagonist turning on its stage in the middle, `Stages/Protagonist.fvscene`), Character (attributes, the modifiers on them, abilities) and Journal (active quests with their visible objectives, finished quests, knowledge). `DisplayNames` gives the names shown for tags and definitions without a display name.
## Build and test

- `dotnet build Bleeders.slnx` and `dotnet test --solution Bleeders.slnx` (Microsoft Testing Platform, xUnit v3), from the repository's root, which has its own `global.json`.
- CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) checks out the engine and builds and tests on Windows and Linux for every pull request and push to main.

## Git

- Branches: `feature/<title>`, `improvement/<title>` or `bug/<title>`, the title saying what the work does.
- PRs open ready for review; the owner reviews and may push to the branch.
